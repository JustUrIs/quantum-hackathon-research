A pattern you can try to break.

The full response, not just a headline.

Cells always refer to A and B, even after their order changes. This prevents a misleading comparison of transposed tables.

How six qubits record two orders

Each order uses one state qubit and two record qubits. Rotate to a question’s basis, copy its answer into a record with CNOT, and rotate back. Keep both records. Final measurements reproduce sequential projection probabilities without measuring halfway through a circuit.

The classical challenger

Does the pattern prove
a quantum mind?

A classical machine that remembers which question came first can reproduce these distributions. Press the button to construct it. Matching QQ does not establish uniquely quantum cognition.

Our challenger uses order-dependent transition probabilities. We fit it to the displayed synthetic distributions. A fit is not an independent prediction.
