A quantum probe can sometimes reveal an object through the way it changes interference—even when the successful probe never enters the object. Every absorbed or lost probe still counts.

What this is: a Qiskit model of a single-photon path interferometer. What it is not: a camera, a human detector, or a new experimental result.

The part that runs on Qollab

An absorber, encoded as a quantum circuit.

One measured circuit starts both object hypotheses in an equal label superposition. The path qubit takes small rotations; a fresh environment qubit records each possible absorption. Once a branch is absorbed, controls prevent it from evolving again.

The ideal statevector is checked against an independent probability formula. The loss slider is deliberately excluded from the circuit and labeled as analytic.

Known physics · careful product claim

Old physics. A better learning instrument.

Interaction-free measurement and high-efficiency quantum interrogation were experimentally demonstrated decades ago. DARKROOM does not claim to invent them. Its contribution is a compact teaching lab that puts coherent interference, sample damage, ordinary loss, and every failed outcome on the same screen.

That makes it useful for teaching quantum measurement and for discussing low-dose sensing. It does not establish a practical imaging or medical device.
