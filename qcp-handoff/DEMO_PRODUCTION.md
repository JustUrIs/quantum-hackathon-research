# Product film: QCP Arena

Target: an English 150-second film. A viewer should understand the task, see a
real error, watch the correction succeed and understand how the product can grow.
QCP is the only project in the film.

## Direction

Use the competitive-programming workspace as the setting: statement, code,
verdict, test report and a running clock. Keep the image quiet enough to read.
Use close framing of the relevant code or result rather than decorative chips,
neon particle effects or a montage of unrelated technology.

The memorable moment is removing one preparation gate and watching the verdict
change. Explain why the edit matters before showing the fix. Avoid treating
Accepted as a magic result that viewers cannot inspect.

Color follows the app: white workspace, blue navigation, green Accepted and red
Wrong Answer. Typography must survive a phone-sized playback. Highlight one
code line at a time. Use hard cuts between tasks and restrained zooms that point
to an action. Keep music below narration, if a free licensed track is available.

## Narration draft

You have a sealed switchboard. Its sensor tells you the parity of whichever
switches you select. Your task is to recover every switch position with one
quantum oracle query.

Here is a circuit that looks close. It fails.

In QCP Arena, you read the problem, write Python and submit your algorithm to a
judge. The judge checks the probability of a correct answer and the resources
you used. You can inspect the case where your solution went wrong.

This solution is missing the target preparation. That operation makes the
oracle write information into relative phases. After the final Hadamards, those
phases determine the answer.

We restore the operation and submit. The reference passes all 124 cases for
this problem. You can open each result, inspect the query budget and download
a report tied to your submitted source.

The complete edition has three problems and 382 public cases. You recover a
hidden string, decide whether a function is constant or balanced, and search
for one marked address.

For the search problem, a correct circuit does not promise the right answer
on every shot. At this example size, two Grover iterations give about 94.5
percent success probability. We check that probability, so a lucky measurement
cannot pass a wrong solution.

You can also enter a one-hour virtual round. Your solved problems and rejected
submissions determine your local score and penalty.

The browser uses a Python builder and an ideal simulator. The Qiskit edition
runs the same problems through a Qollab project. We compare oracle queries;
we do not claim our simulator runs faster than classical algorithms.

Our next step is a pilot with a quantum course or programming club. Open QCP
Arena, attempt the first problem, and inspect what your solution does.

Before recording, verify the Qollab sentence against a real run. If still
pending, replace it with: "We also package the same problems with a Qiskit
judge for Qollab; platform verification is the remaining step." Do not simulate
a live Qollab session in the film.

## Full-AI production workflow, zero budget

1. AI edits narration and timing. Read it aloud with the selected voice and fit
   the real duration to 120–180 seconds; silence and code edits take time.
2. Playwright records the deployed app or a named local execution path. Use an
   isolated browser context and explicit saved fixtures/solutions. Capture the
   verdicts generated by that execution, not fabricated overlays.
3. Generate explanatory overlays with SVG/HTML: the selected gate, phase
   kickback and one-query budget. Identify illustrative graphics as explanation.
4. Generate speech with an available free/offline English TTS voice. Prefer an
   installed system voice or an already available local model. Do not sign up
   for a paid service or promise a particular voice that is unavailable.
5. Assemble with an available renderer: FFmpeg, Remotion or a Python video tool.
   Check installed tools first; keep the rendering source editable. If Remotion
   is chosen, verify that the intended use fits its license and zero budget.
6. Add synchronized captions. Check the names Qiskit, Grover and Qollab, plus
   fractions and probability numbers. Avoid captions covering the relevant code.
7. Review the render with audio, then at phone size without audio. Revise any
   unreadable shot or unexplained result.
8. Export MP4, SRT, narration and source. Check duration, codecs, volume and the
   public no-login viewing link. Only then mark the film delivered.

The full-AI requirement concerns production. The screenshots and results should
come from the working product. Generated visuals must not impersonate evidence.

## Recording fixtures

Read reference solutions from `qcp-qollab/catalog.json`; do not retype them from
memory. For the first failure, remove `qc.x(n)` from A's reference solution.
The starter is another valid failure, but the missing-gate example explains a
specific quantum mistake. Use Run for exploration and Submit for the 124-case
proof. In C, use Run examples to show the n=3, secret=5 probability.

Save judge receipts beside the footage and record the app commit/URL. If a UI
change alters the footage, recapture the relevant shot instead of editing a
number into the old result.

## Delivery checklist

- A viewer understands what the student writes and what the judge checks.
- The first error and correction are real.
- No fake users, global rank, noisy-hardware result or customer claim appears.
- The probability and query comparisons agree with the implementation.
- Qollab footage uses a real verified session, or the limitation is stated.
- Captions, audio and focused UI remain legible through the final export.
- The film and its opening URL feature only QCP.
